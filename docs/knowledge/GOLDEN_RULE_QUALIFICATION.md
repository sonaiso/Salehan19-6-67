# GOLDEN RULE QUALIFICATION

## Definitions

- **OrdinaryPriorRule**: prior information that can help ranking or interpretation.
- **GoldenRule**: scoped governing prior that can block or permit certificate ascent.
- **GoldenRuleQualificationGate**: deterministic gate that evaluates whether a prior rule is mature enough to govern judgments.
- **CertaintyLevel**: explicit certainty class (G0 constitutional, G1 formal, G2 computational, G3 empirical-under-conditions, G4 linguistic-normative, G5 contextual usage, G6 hypothesis prior).
- **ScopeCompleteness**: rule scope is explicit (domain, layer, conditions) and not universalized outside its boundary.
- **EvidenceCompleteness**: required evidence is explicit and testable.
- **CertificateBlockingPower**: rule can block unjustified CERTIFICATE ascent.
- **ResidualExpectation**: rule defines residuals that must be emitted/preserved when obligations are unmet.
- **ForbiddenTransitionPower**: rule defines transitions that must never be allowed.
- **ConceptAdmissibility**: new concept claim must be evaluated against qualified golden rules before admissibility.
- **GoldenRuleMaturityLevel**: `DRAFT_PRIOR -> SCOPED_PRIOR -> GOVERNING_PRIOR -> GOLDEN_RULE_CANDIDATE -> GOLDEN_RULE`.

## Why metrics alone are insufficient

Metrics are post-hoc. They measure outcomes after decisions, but they do not define preconditions for certificate governance. Without qualified golden rules, metrics can report low errors while still allowing structurally invalid certificate ascent.

## Why prior knowledge must be qualified before governing

Not every prior is governing law. Qualification ensures a prior has scope, certainty declaration, evidence obligations, certificate blockers, forbidden transitions, residual expectations, reverse trace requirements, and tests/benchmarks.

## Why rule-without-scope cannot be golden

A scope-less rule overgeneralizes and can silently over-certify unrelated domains. Golden rules must explicitly bind where they apply and where they do not.

## Why empirical and linguistic rules are scoped, not absolute

Empirical rules require measurement conditions and can be blocked by alternative explanations. Linguistic rules can support strong interpretations but remain context-sensitive and may preserve residuals that block certificate.

## Golden rules as measuring instrument for new concepts

Every new concept claim is evaluated by:

`NewConcept -> GoldenRuleMatch -> EvidenceCheck -> ResidualCheck -> ForbiddenTransitionCheck -> Judgment`

If obligations fail, certificate ascent is blocked and residuals are emitted. This makes golden rules an expandable domain governance system: each domain can define its own governing golden rules while preserving constitutional constraints.

## Qualification Gate Requirements

A rule can reach **GOLDEN_RULE** only if all are explicit:

- scope
- certainty level
- evidence requirements
- certificate blockers
- forbidden transitions (where relevant)
- residual expectations
- reverse trace requirements
- at least one test or benchmark reference
